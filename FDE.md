## Recommended position

**Forward-deployed engineering is an embedded, outcome-led way of turning high-value operational problems into production systems—and leaving the client able to own, operate, and extend what was built.**

The website should lead with the client problem and outcome, not with the unfamiliar job title. “Forward-deployed engineering” can name the method after the value is clear.

### North-star line

**From critical workflow to working system—and a team that can carry it forward.**

### Core value proposition

We embed a small, senior engineering team with the people who know the work. Together, we define the outcome, build in the real operating environment, prove the system under real constraints, and transfer the knowledge and tools needed to keep improving it.

Clients should leave with three forms of value:

1. **A measurable operational result**, not only a recommendation or prototype.
2. **A production-capable system**, with the tests, controls, and operating evidence needed to trust it.
3. **Greater internal capability**, so the client can operate, evaluate, and extend the system without permanent dependence on us.

## The story the website should tell

### The problem

The hardest part of deploying new technology is rarely access to the technology itself. The gap appears between a promising capability and the way a real organization works: fragmented systems, tacit judgment, security requirements, edge cases, adoption, and competing priorities.

Traditional advisory work can stop at a roadmap. Staff augmentation can add hands without owning the outcome. A proof of concept can demonstrate possibility without surviving contact with production.

### The response

Forward-deployed engineers work inside that gap. They learn the workflow from operators, translate business judgment into testable system behavior, write production code alongside the client team, and use evidence from real use to improve both the solution and the way it is operated.

### The durable result

The engagement is complete when the system works, the result can be measured, and the client has the context, controls, and confidence to carry it forward. Good embedded engineering reduces dependency over time.

## Message hierarchy

Use this order on the page:

1. **Outcome:** Put useful systems into production around a meaningful business result.
2. **Method:** Embed with operators and engineers; build and learn in the real environment.
3. **Trust:** Validate behavior, security, reliability, and adoption with evidence.
4. **Ownership:** Transfer the operating capability, not just the code.
5. **Compounding value:** Turn field learning into reusable patterns without turning every client into a bespoke maintenance obligation.

## How we work

**Heading**  
Embedded from problem framing through operation.

### 1. Frame the outcome

We start with the operational result, the people affected, and the current baseline. We separate the problem from the proposed solution and define how value and risk will be measured.

### 2. Learn the work in context

We work directly with operators, domain experts, engineers, security teams, and system owners to uncover the rules, exceptions, constraints, and tacit judgment that documentation alone misses.

### 3. Prove a thin slice

We put the smallest useful end-to-end capability into the real workflow, with explicit acceptance criteria and representative cases. The goal is evidence, not theater.

### 4. Productionize what works

We harden the system for integration, security, reliability, observability, governance, and support. Findings become tested changes, not an ever-growing collection of workarounds.

### 5. Transfer and compound

We enable internal owners, rehearse operations and recovery, and leave behind the code, tests, decisions, runbooks, and improvement loop. The client owns the outcome and the capability to keep learning.

## Differentiators

### We own outcomes, not activity

Success is tied to an agreed operational result and the evidence that demonstrates it—not hours billed, tickets closed, code produced, or pilots launched.

### We build with the people who do the work

Domain expertise is not a discovery artifact collected at the beginning. Operators and technical owners remain part of the design, evaluation, and improvement loop.

### Strategy and implementation stay connected

The people framing the architecture remain close to the build and its production evidence. This reduces the handoff loss between recommendation, implementation, and operation.

### Governance is part of engineering

Security, risk, human oversight, evaluation, and change control are designed into the workflow from the start and scaled to the consequence of failure.

### Independence is a deliverable

We plan knowledge transfer, internal ownership, and exit criteria at the beginning. Our aim is to make the client more capable with every release.

### Field learning becomes leverage

Each engagement should improve the next through reusable patterns and better methods. Reuse never substitutes for learning the client’s actual environment.

## What the client keeps

Depending on the engagement, the client should retain:

- Production code and infrastructure definitions under agreed IP terms
- Architecture and decision records
- Requirements and acceptance criteria linked to their sources
- Representative test cases and evaluation suites
- Security, privacy, and risk controls
- Telemetry, dashboards, alerts, and operating thresholds
- Runbooks, recovery procedures, and escalation paths
- Known limitations and a prioritized improvement backlog
- Training, pairing, and teach-back materials
- A named internal owner and an agreed operating cadence

## Best-fit engagements

This model is strongest when:

- The outcome matters enough to require production-grade engineering.
- The path to the result is not fully known at the start.
- Important knowledge is distributed across business, operations, data, security, and technology teams.
- The system must fit existing workflows and constraints rather than live as an isolated demo.
- Fast learning is valuable, but reliability, governance, and ownership cannot be postponed.
- The client wants to build durable internal capability, not rent a permanent translation layer.

## When this is not the right model

Be explicit about poor fit. Forward-deployed engineering is probably unnecessary when:

- The requirement is already clear and a conventional delivery team can implement it predictably.
- An off-the-shelf product solves the need with routine configuration.
- The client needs temporary coding capacity but not embedded discovery or outcome ownership.
- There is no empowered process owner, access to representative users, or credible route to production.
- The work is a low-consequence experiment with no present intent to operationalize.

## FAQ copy

### What is forward-deployed engineering?

It is an embedded delivery model for problems where technology must be adapted to a real operating environment. Engineers work directly with domain experts and internal technical teams to define the outcome, build the system, put it into production, and learn from actual use.

### How is this different from traditional consulting?

Traditional consulting often separates advice from implementation. Our model keeps problem framing, architecture, hands-on engineering, validation, and adoption in one accountable loop. The engagement is judged by working outcomes and operational readiness, not by the delivery of a roadmap alone.

### How is this different from staff augmentation?

Staff augmentation primarily adds capacity to work the client has already defined. A forward-deployed team is responsible for reducing ambiguity, connecting business and technical decisions, shipping a bounded production capability, and building the client’s ability to own it.

### Is this only for AI?

No. The model applies whenever the main difficulty is translating an important operational problem into a dependable system. AI makes the need more visible because behavior is probabilistic, context matters greatly, and production quality cannot be inferred from a demo.

### Will we become dependent on your team?

The opposite is a design goal. We pair with internal owners, preserve decisions and evidence, build client-controlled tests and runbooks, and define operating independence as an exit condition. The client should be more self-sufficient when the engagement ends.

### Are you tied to a particular model or platform?

**Use only if true:** We recommend and integrate technology based on the client’s outcome, constraints, and existing environment. We can work with preferred platforms while preserving clear interfaces, evaluation evidence, and informed choices about lock-in.

### How do you measure success?

We agree on measures before building. These normally include a business or operational result, production quality and risk measures, real adoption, time to useful capability, and evidence that the internal team can operate and extend the system.

## Proof architecture

The copy will become credible when paired with specific evidence. For each case study, collect:

1. **Before:** Baseline performance, constraint, or failure mode.
2. **Intervention:** The workflow and system boundary changed.
3. **Production evidence:** Where, with whom, and under what conditions it ran.
4. **Outcome:** A measured change over a stated period.
5. **Trust evidence:** Quality, risk, reliability, or control results.
6. **Transfer evidence:** What the client can now operate or change without the consultancy.
7. **Caveat:** What remains unknown, limited, or outside scope.

Useful proof measures include:

- Cycle time, throughput, cost per case, revenue, or avoided loss
- Percentage of representative cases handled correctly
- Adoption and sustained use by the intended operators
- Evaluation pass rate, incident rate, escalation rate, and recovery time
- Time from approved problem to first useful production release
- Percentage of operational issues resolved by the client team
- Time for a client engineer to make and safely deploy a representative change
- Reuse of tested components or patterns without client-specific leakage

Avoid publishing unsupported claims such as “10× faster,” “production in days,” “fully autonomous,” “guaranteed ROI,” or “eliminates hallucinations.”

## Voice and vocabulary

### Prefer

- Embedded, alongside, co-build
- Outcome, workflow, operating reality
- Production-capable, tested, observable
- Evidence, representative cases, measurable
- Human judgment, clear ownership, safe escalation
- Transfer, self-sufficiency, internal champions
- Reusable patterns, client-owned capability

### Use carefully

- **Frontier:** Can sound fashionable or imply privileged access to a model lab. Pair it with a concrete definition.
- **Transformation:** Name the workflow and measurable change; avoid using it as an outcome by itself.
- **Agentic:** Use only when the system genuinely chooses steps or tools. Many valuable systems are deterministic workflows with model-assisted steps.
- **Autonomous:** State scope, controls, and escalation boundaries.
- **Production-ready:** Prefer “production-capable” until deployment, load, security, recovery, and operational ownership have been demonstrated.

### Avoid

- Generic “AI strategy” without a path to production
- “Resources,” “bodies,” or other staff-augmentation framing
- “Best practices” without context or evidence
- “Seamless,” “turnkey,” “future-proof,” and “revolutionary”
- Language suggesting that internal teams are the obstacle
- Positioning dependency or permanent bespoke work as a premium feature

## Claims and structural guardrails

1. **Do not imply model-vendor access unless a formal relationship exists.** Vendor FDEs can work directly with their own product and research teams; an independent consultancy offers different strengths.
2. **Be clear about platform independence.** If the practice is vendor-neutral, say so and show how decisions are made. If it is platform-aligned, make the alignment an explicit advantage and disclose the boundary.
3. **Do not confuse proximity with discovery theater.** Embedded work means shared decisions, code, production evidence, and ownership—not simply placing consultants on site.
4. **Do not promise a fixed method for every risk level.** Preserve evidence, review, and verification principles while scaling the ceremony to the work.
5. **Make exit part of the offer.** A client’s growing independence is proof of success, not lost revenue.
6. **Protect both forms of IP.** The client retains its code, data, operating context, and domain-specific instructions under the agreement; the consultancy retains only clearly defined, non-client-specific methods and reusable assets.

This brief synthesizes the following sources; the phrasing above is original and should not be presented as an endorsement by any source organization.

- [Frontier Engineering Core](https://github.com/frontier-engineering/fe-core/tree/71511b59dc31ec6e75a50da03eece066ea403095) emphasizes evidence-backed specification, implementation, and operations; bounded sessions; human judgment; traceable decisions; and verified outcomes.
- [Microsoft HVE Core](https://microsoft.github.io/hve-core/docs/hve-guide/) distinguishes the full project lifecycle from the Research–Plan–Implement–Review task loop and preserves durable evidence across handoffs.
- [Anthropic’s Claude Frontier Academy](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-frontier-academy) frames the role as hands-on production leadership that spans use-case selection, security review, deployment, and handover, developed through practice on real cases.
- [“The FDE boom is a product gap”](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/fde-boom-product-gap-eugen-alpeza-bbfhc/) is a useful warning: an engineer should not remain the permanent translator between operators and systems. Operational judgment should become client-owned, tested, versioned instructions and an improvement loop.
- [OpenAI’s FDE description](https://openai.com/business/forward-deployed-engineering/) centers outcomes, co-building, production use, and packaging successful workflows into repeatable capabilities.
- [Palantir’s architecture overview](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/architecture-center) describes field engineering as a feedback mechanism between demanding operating environments and core product engineering.
- [Cohere on capability rather than dependency](https://cohere.com/blog/forward-deployed-engineers-capability-building) makes operational independence a first-class result and identifies tests, troubleshooting ability, and knowledge transfer as the means.
- [AWS Partner-Led Forward Deployed Engineering](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/apn/introducing-forward-deployed-engineering-for-partners-winning-the-future-of-enterprise-ai/) explicitly applies the model to consulting partners, with production standards, reusable delivery assets, and customer self-sufficiency.
- [NIST AI RMF Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/) supports continuous governance, contextual mapping, measurement, and management throughout the system lifecycle.
- [Anthropic on building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents) supports starting with the simplest viable architecture and adding autonomy only when it demonstrably improves the outcome.
